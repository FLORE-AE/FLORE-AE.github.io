01 Method

Latent audio generation needs an autoencoder whose compact latents are easy to model and still decode to faithful, realistic waveforms.

MotivationFrom spectral–adversarial to waveform diffusion decoding

The prevailing paradigm · spectral–adversarial decoding

A deterministic decoder maps each latent to one waveform. Spectral losses supervise correspondence to the source, and a jointly trained discriminator supplies realism.

  • Minimax training. The decoder chases an evolving critic; adversarial updates can be unstable, and representation learning is tied to the real/fake objective.
  • Indirect detail. Fine detail the latent leaves open is shaped only through spectral and real/fake feedback.
  • Rate pressure. Keeping more detail means a larger latent, which makes downstream generation more expensive.
FLORE-AE · waveform diffusion decoding

Decoding becomes latent-conditioned generation: the latent encodes source structure, and a diffusion decoder generates the waveform from noise.

  • Regression, no critic. The decoder is trained by clean-target regression along noise–data paths, with spectral reconstruction as additional supervision.
  • Detail as residual variation. Acoustic detail the latent leaves open is modeled by the decoder rather than shaped indirectly.
  • Compact, continuous latents. The latent need not grow to specify every sample, and the downstream generator models continuous latents rather than quantized codec tokens.

FLORE-AE objective · regression only

ChallengesThree obstacles to direct diffusion decoding

Swapping in a standard waveform diffusion decoder does not work out of the box: at 2048–4096× compression, three obstacles appear.

  1. ①

    Wide dynamic range

    A diffusion decoder applies one noise schedule to the whole waveform, so quiet passages are buried in noise long before loud ones. Normalizing evens this out but discards the dynamics the reconstruction must restore.

    Core issue

    Loudness shifts the effective noise level

    Goal

    A scale-invariant denoising target, with amplitude kept in the latent.

    → A · Carrier–amplitude factorization
  2. ②

    Sparse-to-dense synthesis

    Each latent step must steer thousands of samples. Waveform U-Nets add multiscale structure but reach the latent only through intermediate features; DiTs model context with attention but compute every layer on the finest grid.

    Core issue

    Latents are 2,048–4,096× shorter than the waveform

    Goal

    Time-aligned latent guidance at every scale, with broad context on coarser grids.

    → B · FLORE-DiT
  3. ③

    Iterative decoding cost

    Diffusion decoders call the network several times per clip, while spectral–adversarial decoders need one pass. This latency offsets the gain from compact latents, and fewer steps usually cost quality on generated latents.

    Core issue

    Each sampling step costs a full decoder pass

    Goal

    Two-step decoding that holds on generated as well as clean latents.

    → C · Endpoint-Aware MeanFlow

ApproachHow FLORE-AE addresses them

  1. A · addresses ①

    Carrier–amplitude factorization

    Diffuse only the carrier normalized by a smoothed local-RMS envelope, so all regions share a similar noise level; an amplitude predictor recovers global gain and local dynamics from the same latent, so the latent must still encode them.

    Global RMS normalization lowers rFAD but costs SI-SDR and MEL; only the local carrier improves all four metrics, and inverse-energy loss weighting does not reproduce it. Normalize what hampers denoising, but make the latent reconstruct the dynamics.

  2. B · addresses ②

    FLORE-DiT

    A DiT with a downsampling–upsampling hierarchy: each latent step is expanded into position-specific conditions and re-injected at every resolution, keeping source guidance time-aligned, while broader context moves to coarser stages with sliding-window attention.

    A standard DiT already has the best rFAD (0.108) but only 10.07 dB SI-SDR: plausible audio is not a faithful reconstruction. The aligned path raises SI-SDR on every backbone, with the learned expansion the most clearly supported component; the hierarchy then keeps quality at 64% lower inner-DiT compute.

  4. C · addresses ③

    Endpoint-Aware MeanFlow

    Adapt the decoder to finite-interval average velocities (iMF) and add spectral supervision of instantaneous and near-endpoint predictions; decoding takes two updates on the grid (0, 0.5, 1).

    Halving evaluations with iMF improves FAD only modestly (0.118 → 0.111); endpoint-aware supervision at the same two evaluations lowers it to 0.086. Decoder objectives should be judged on generated latents under the deployed interface.

Training and inference

Stage 1 trains the encoder, carrier decoder and amplitude predictor jointly with a KL bottleneck; Stage 2 freezes the encoder and adapts the decoder with EA-MF. Reconstruction, generation and editing share this decoder.
